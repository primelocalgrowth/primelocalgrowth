What runs on its own

  • Rebooking and fill-the-calendar messages go out on their own. From October 1 to 23, the booking platform tied 11 reservations to those automated messages. That is the platform's own attribution, not a verified count of completed visits.
  • A review-request routine keeps new reviews coming. Google reviews went from 47 to 92 while the profile recovered.
  • The new website tracks booking clicks. Every tap on the reservation button is recorded, so we can see which pages lead to bookings and fix the ones that do not.
  • A scheduled check watches the profile. Reviews, hours and the reservation link are reviewed on a schedule, so a problem does not sit unnoticed for weeks.
  • A monthly report shows every number with its source and date range. If a number is not moving, the report says so.

How we decide

Every tactic starts with the numbers, not a guess.

For the same anonymized client, we pull Google, the booking platform, website analytics and search data each month, reconcile them, and let the gaps pick the next move. The analysis is automated and AI-assisted, and Adam reviews every conclusion before it goes in a report.

Booked sales, October vs September

What the data showed: Sales rose from one month to the next over the same 20 working days, and services performed rose 6.6%. Sales had dipped 12.3% in the third quarter when one of two barbers left, and a single month does not prove a trend.

What we did with it: We report the dips next to the gains, and we avoid crediting marketing for a month that was also shaped by the shop's schedule.

Leak 1
1 in 5

Appointments never marked complete

What the data showed: About one in five appointments were never marked as completed in the booking system, so reports undercounted real visits.

What we did with it: We flagged it for the shop and treat service counts with that caveat until outcomes are marked daily, so we never act on a number we do not trust.

Leak 2
-38%

New clients, same-length windows

What the data showed: New clients fell from 26 to 16 between matching 22-day windows, and only 34% of new clients came back for a second visit, versus 51% of existing clients.

What we did with it: Automated rebooking and fill-the-calendar messages now go out on their own. We will check the second-visit rate next.

Why this works

We work on the things Google says it ranks.

Google's own help pages say local results come from three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Here is how each one maps to our work, and what it looked like for the client above.

Relevance

What Google says: Complete, detailed business information helps your profile match what people search for, and businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up.

What we do about it: We keep hours, categories, services and the reservation link accurate and check them on a schedule. For this client we also track every tap on the booking button, so we can see what the profile and website actually produce.

Prominence

What Google says: More reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, and helpful replies to reviews can help a business stand out.

What we do about it: We set up a simple review-request routine for real customers. This client went from 47 to 92 Google reviews and reached the number one Maps spot for a tracked search in July.

Distance

What Google says: Google considers how far each business is from the person searching.

What we do about it: We cannot move your shop, and nobody honest can. We make sure that when someone nearby searches, your profile is the one that earns the call, the visit or the click.
